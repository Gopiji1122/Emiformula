Gold Loan Guide: Gold Value, Loan Amount, EMI and Interest

A gold loan lets you borrow against eligible gold jewellery or other accepted gold collateral. The amount a lender offers depends on its valuation method, eligible net gold weight, purity, applicable loan-to-value rules, product terms and repayment method. Use this guide to understand the estimates before comparing a lender’s written offer.

How to use a gold loan calculator

A gold loan calculator provides an estimate based on the details you enter. Depending on the tool, inputs may include gold weight, purity, price per gram, loan amount, interest rate, tenure and repayment method. Check the input labels carefully: a price quoted for 24K gold should not be treated as the price of 22K jewellery without a purity adjustment.

Calculator results are indicative, not a promise of approval or a live market quote. Confirm the current rate, eligible weight, valuation deductions, fees and repayment conditions with the lender.

How much loan can you get for 1 gram, 10 grams or 40 grams of gold?

There is no single guaranteed gold-loan amount per gram. It changes with the assessed gold value, purity, eligible net weight, lender policy and applicable regulatory limits. A 10-gram or 40-gram estimate should therefore be calculated from the lender’s accepted valuation basis rather than a generic online rate.

Illustrative method: eligible gold value = accepted net weight × assessed price per gram. An indicative loan ceiling may then be estimated using the applicable LTV percentage. This is only a planning method; actual assessment can differ.

Calculation method

Eligible gold value = accepted net weight × assessed price per gram.

Indicative loan amount depends on applicable LTV and lender assessment.

Gold purity, per-gram price and valuation

Purity indicates how much of the item’s weight is gold. 24K is purer than 22K or 18K, but jewellery valuation also depends on the lender’s accepted test, net gold weight and exclusions such as stones or non-gold components. Do not assume the retail jewellery bill or making charges equal the collateral value.

If a calculator asks for a rate per gram for the selected purity, enter a rate on that same basis. If it asks for a 24K reference rate, the calculation needs a clear purity adjustment. Avoid mixing these two input methods.

Gold loan LTV and eligibility

Loan-to-value (LTV) compares the loan amount with the value accepted for the pledged gold. A lender may offer less than the maximum permitted amount after its valuation and product checks. The applicable rules can depend on loan purpose, amount, lender and current regulations, so avoid treating one LTV percentage as universal.

Ask the lender how it determines net weight, purity, assessed price, eligible amount, charges and any margin. Verify current official requirements and the loan agreement before pledging jewellery.

Gold loan EMI and interest calculation

For a regular reducing-balance EMI loan, the instalment depends on principal, periodic interest rate and number of payments. A common EMI formula is EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n ÷ ((1+r)^n − 1), where P is principal, r is the rate per payment period and n is the number of payments. This formula does not describe every gold-loan product.

Some products use bullet repayment, periodic interest payments or other structures. Fees, overdue charges, renewal terms and the timing of interest may change the total cost. Match the calculator’s repayment method to the actual lender offer.

Bullet repayment and gold mortgage loans

With a bullet structure, principal and interest may be due at maturity (capped at 12 months for consumption loans under RBI’s 2025 Directions, where the 85% / 80% / 75% LTV ceiling is calculated on the total maturity payable amount including interest). Eligible pledged collateral per borrower is also capped at 1 kilogram for gold ornaments and 50 grams for gold coins. “Gold mortgage loan” and “loan against gold jewellery” are commonly used descriptions; read the actual product terms rather than relying on the name.

Gold loan calculation formula: a worked approach

Step 1: determine the net gold weight accepted by the lender. Step 2: establish the assessed price per gram for the tested purity. Step 3: estimate the collateral value. Step 4: apply the applicable LTV limit and lender eligibility checks. Step 5: estimate interest and repayment using the actual product’s method and charges.

For example, if 10 grams are accepted at an assessed value of ₹X per gram, the indicative assessed value is 10 × ₹X. Do not substitute a made-up live gold price: enter a verified rate and check whether it is for the same purity and valuation basis.

Common questions about gold loans

A per-gram amount is an estimate, not a guaranteed rate. Interest can be calculated on a reducing balance, as a periodic charge or under another product-specific method. The correct way to calculate interest is to use the repayment structure in the lender’s offer.

For location-specific or lender-specific searches such as DCB or IIFL, check that institution’s current official terms and rate card. This guide does not represent any lender and does not publish live rates.

Frequently asked questions

How much loan can I get for 10 grams of gold?

It depends on the assessed value of the eligible net gold weight, purity, lender policy and applicable LTV rules. Use a current verified rate and treat the calculator result as an estimate.

How do I calculate gold loan interest?

Use the rate, balance method, payment schedule and fees in the lender’s terms. A standard reducing-balance EMI formula may not apply to bullet or other repayment products.

What does LTV mean in a gold loan?

LTV is the loan amount compared with the accepted value of the pledged gold. The permitted limit and actual offer can differ by applicable rules and lender assessment.

Can I use a 24K gold rate for 22K jewellery?

Not without a clear purity adjustment. Confirm whether the entered rate is a 24K reference price or a rate already quoted for 22K gold.

Is the online gold loan amount guaranteed?

No. The lender’s final assessment, eligible weight, purity test, current rules and contract terms determine the actual offer.

Important note

Estimates are for education and planning, not a loan offer or financial advice. Gold rates and lender terms change. Verify current applicable RBI requirements, the lender’s official rate card and the signed loan agreement before making a decision.
