01

What is a personal loan?

A personal loan is money borrowed from a bank, financial institution or other lender that you agree to repay under a set of loan terms, usually with interest.

Unlike a home or vehicle loan, a personal loan is generally not tied to one specific asset. Depending on the lender and product, people may use personal loans for home repairs, education expenses, medical costs, weddings, travel, emergencies or other permitted needs.

The exact purpose allowed, interest rate, fees and eligibility conditions depend on the lender and loan product.

02

How does a personal loan work?

You apply for a loan amount, the lender assesses the application under its own criteria, and an approved loan is then provided according to the agreed terms.

You repay the loan through scheduled payments. For a standard EMI-based loan, each payment generally contains a portion that reduces the principal and a portion that covers interest.

As the principal falls, the outstanding balance changes. When the required balance and charges have been settled according to the agreement, the loan is closed through the lender's process.

04

What is the principal?

The principal is the amount you borrow before interest and other applicable charges.

If you borrow ₹3,75,000, that is your starting principal. As you make repayments, part of each payment generally reduces the outstanding principal.

The balance may move from ₹3,75,000 to a lower amount month after month. The exact path depends on the interest rate, EMI, payment timing and lender's calculation method.

05

What is personal loan interest?

Interest is the cost charged by the lender for providing the borrowed money.

For a reducing-balance EMI loan, interest is generally linked to the outstanding principal according to the lender's method. Because the balance changes as you repay, the interest portion can change as well.

Interest is only one part of the comparisonCheck the rate, fees, tenure and total repayment together rather than choosing from the headline rate alone.

What is loan tenure?

Loan tenure is the amount of time you have agreed to take to repay the loan.

3 years36 monthly payments
5 years60 monthly payments
7 years84 monthly payments

Tenure is important because it affects both your monthly payment and the total interest. A longer period generally lowers the scheduled EMI but can increase the total interest in a standard reducing-balance loan.

08

Short tenure vs long tenure

Shorter tenureUsually higher EMI · Faster repayment · Often less total interest
Longer tenureUsually lower EMI · More time to repay · Often more total interest

There is no single tenure that suits everyone. The useful comparison is whether the monthly payment is manageable while the total borrowing cost remains reasonable for your situation.

09

What is a processing fee?

A processing fee is a charge a lender may apply for processing a loan application. It can be a fixed amount or a percentage of the loan, depending on the lender.

For example, if a lender charges 1.5% on a ₹4,00,000 loan, the basic fee would be ₹6,000 before any applicable taxes or other charges.

This is why two loans with similar interest rates can still have different overall costs.

10

Loan amount vs amount you actually receive

The approved loan amount and the amount credited to your bank account are not always identical.

Suppose a loan is approved for ₹4,00,000 and an applicable ₹6,000 charge is deducted from the disbursement. The amount credited could be ₹3,94,000.

The exact treatment depends on the lender and agreement. Before accepting the loan, check both the approved amount and the actual disbursement amount.

11

What is the real cost of a personal loan?

The interest rate is important, but it may not tell you the complete cost.

Depending on the product, you may also have processing fees, documentation charges, insurance costs, prepayment or foreclosure charges, taxes or other lender-specific costs.

A practical comparison therefore looks at:

Interest

How much the borrowing costs over the repayment period.

Upfront charges

Fees that may be paid or deducted at the beginning.

Other charges

Costs that can apply during the loan according to its terms.

Total repayment

The scheduled payments over the full tenure.

13

Fixed and variable interest rates

A fixed-rate arrangement generally keeps the agreed rate stable for the applicable fixed period or according to the product's terms.

A variable or floating rate can change under the loan agreement. When the rate changes, the lender may adjust the EMI, remaining tenure or both, depending on its rules.

Before accepting a variable-rate loan, understand what happens if the rate rises or falls.

14

What happens if you pay extra?

Some borrowers make extra payments when they receive a bonus, refund or other additional income. If the lender allows it, an extra payment towards principal can reduce the outstanding balance sooner.

On a reducing-balance loan, a lower balance can reduce future interest. The actual benefit depends on the lender's rules and how the repayment schedule is recalculated.

15

Part-prepayment and foreclosure

Part-prepayment

You pay an additional amount while keeping the loan active. This can reduce the outstanding principal, subject to the lender's rules.

Full prepayment or foreclosure

You repay the outstanding amount before the scheduled end of the tenure and close the loan. The lender may have a specific closure process and applicable charges.

Do not assume an early payment is automatically free. Check the agreement and ask the lender for the official amount and conditions.

16

How to check whether the EMI fits your budget

A lender's approved amount is not the same thing as an amount that is comfortable for your household budget.

Before borrowing, consider your income and regular commitments such as rent, existing EMIs, food, transport, insurance, education, savings and unexpected expenses.

The goal is not simply to find the biggest loan you can qualify for. It is to understand whether the new payment can fit alongside your normal life.

17

Compare the same loan at different rates

Suppose you need ₹5,25,000 for 5 years. Instead of calculating only one rate, you could test 10.5%, 11.5% and 12.5% and compare the resulting EMI and total interest.

The rate actually available to you depends on the lender and your eligibility. The point of the comparison is to see how even a modest rate difference can affect the total cost.

Run the comparison with your own loan details before relying on a single quote.

18

Compare different loan amounts and tenures

Sometimes the most useful comparison is not between lenders but between borrowing choices.

For example, if your planned expense is ₹6,00,000, you could compare borrowing ₹6,00,000, ₹5,00,000 and ₹4,50,000 if the remaining amount can be covered without weakening your emergency savings.

You can also compare 36, 48, 60 and 72 months. Watch both the EMI and total interest. This shows the real trade-off between monthly cash flow and total borrowing cost.

19

What about debt consolidation?

Debt consolidation means replacing multiple debts with one new loan. The attraction can be simpler repayment management, but consolidation does not automatically make debt cheaper.

Compare the remaining cost of the old debts with the new loan's interest, fees and repayment period. A new loan can have a lower monthly payment simply because it stretches repayment over more time.

If you consolidate debt, also consider what caused the original balances and whether the new payment fits your regular budget.

20

What happens if you miss an EMI?

Missing a scheduled payment can have consequences under the loan agreement. Depending on the lender and product, these may include late-payment charges, additional costs or an impact on your credit history.

If you think you may have trouble making a payment, check the lender's terms and contact the lender rather than ignoring the problem.

21

Personal loans and your credit history

Loan and repayment activity can be reported to credit bureaus. Making required payments on time is important for maintaining a healthy credit record.

A personal loan does not automatically improve or damage your credit profile. What matters is how the borrowing is managed and the information reported by lenders.

Common personal loan mistakes

Choosing only by interest rate

Fees and other terms can change the overall cost.

Choosing only by EMI

A lower EMI can come from a longer tenure.

Borrowing more than needed

A higher approved amount does not mean you need to use it.

Ignoring the amount actually received

Upfront deductions can make the credited amount lower than the approved amount.

Ignoring early-repayment rules

Check the conditions before assuming you can close the loan without a charge.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about personal loans

Clear answers to common questions people ask before taking or comparing a personal loan.

What is a personal loan?

A personal loan is money borrowed from a lender that is repaid according to agreed terms, usually through regular payments that include principal and interest.

How is a personal loan EMI calculated?

For a standard reducing-balance loan, EMI depends mainly on the loan amount, monthly interest rate and number of monthly payments. Lenders may use their own calculation and rounding methods.

Does a longer personal loan tenure reduce EMI?

Generally, yes. A longer tenure usually lowers the scheduled monthly payment for the same loan amount and interest rate, but it can increase total interest.

What is a processing fee?

A processing fee is a charge a lender may apply for processing a loan application. It may be a fixed amount or a percentage of the loan, depending on the lender.

Does the processing fee reduce the amount I receive?

It can, if the lender deducts the fee from the loan disbursement. The exact treatment depends on the loan agreement.

Can I pay extra on a personal loan?

Some loans allow additional payments, while others have conditions or charges. Check the lender's agreement before making extra payments.

What is prepayment?

Prepayment means paying part or all of the outstanding loan before the originally scheduled repayment date.

What is foreclosure?

Foreclosure or early closure generally means paying the outstanding loan before the scheduled end of the tenure. The lender may apply specific charges or procedures.

Is a lower EMI always better?

No. A lower EMI can result from a longer tenure, which may increase the total interest paid.

Can a personal loan EMI change?

It depends on the interest-rate arrangement and loan terms. A variable-rate loan may result in changes to the EMI, tenure, or both.

Are online personal loan calculators accurate?

They can provide useful estimates based on the information entered. The lender's official calculation can differ because of payment dates, fees, rounding, rate changes and loan-specific terms.

What should I check before accepting a personal loan?

Check the interest rate, EMI, tenure, total interest, processing fee, other charges, amount disbursed, prepayment rules, late-payment terms and the complete loan agreement.
