SHG Loan Guide: Member Loans, Reducing Interest and CCL Planning

Self-Help Groups (SHGs) commonly manage regular savings, internal lending, repayments and records. This guide explains calculation methods; it does not replace your group's approved rules, bank terms or current official circulars.

Equal principal with reducing interest

Divide the loan principal across the planned months. Each month, calculate interest on the opening outstanding principal. The total instalment falls as the balance reduces, provided principal payments are regular.

Monthly interest = opening outstanding principal × monthly interest rate.

Constant EMI

A constant EMI keeps the scheduled payment approximately equal each month. The interest share generally falls over time and the principal share rises. This method is common in structured bank repayment schedules.

Flat interest

Flat interest is calculated on the original loan amount for each period, even when principal is repaid. It is not equivalent to reducing-balance interest, so a comparison should label the method clearly.

Irregular principal payments

If a member pauses principal or pays a different amount, the later outstanding balance and interest must be recalculated. The SHG calculator allows principal edits in its schedule. It assumes the entered principal amount is applied at the end of each monthly period and calculates interest on the opening balance; actual group rules may differ.

Group corpus and bank linkage

Corpus estimates depend on which balances are included, whether loans outstanding are treated as group assets, grants received, liabilities, and current scheme rules. The calculator separates estimated savings, net assets and cash-like funds and shows CCL figures only as indicative planning estimates. Banks determine actual sanction and drawing power after appraisal under current rules.
