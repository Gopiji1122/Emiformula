SHG Loan Guide: Member Loans, Reducing Interest and CCL Planning

Self-Help Groups (SHGs) manage regular savings, internal lending, repayments and records. This guide explains the calculation methods with a worked ₹50,000 example. It does not replace your group's approved rules, bank terms or current official circulars.

What an SHG member loan is

An SHG member loan is money lent from the group's pooled savings to one of its members, repaid over an agreed number of months with interest. The interest usually stays inside the group and builds its corpus. Three choices decide how much a member pays: the interest rate, the repayment method and the tenure.

Every example below uses the same loan so the methods can be compared fairly: ₹50,000 for 10 months at 12% a year (1% a month).

Equal principal with reducing interest

Divide the loan principal equally across the months. Each month, charge interest only on the principal still outstanding at the start of that month. The instalment therefore falls steadily, as long as principal is repaid on schedule.

FormulaMonthly interest = opening outstanding principal × monthly rate

Monthly principal = ₹50,000 ÷ 10 = ₹5,000. Month-one interest = ₹50,000 × 1% = ₹500, so the first instalment is ₹5,500.

Constant EMI method

A constant EMI keeps the monthly payment the same. Early payments carry more interest and less principal; later ones carry less interest and more principal. This is the method most banks use for structured repayment schedules.

FormulaEMI = P × r × (1+r)^n ÷ ((1+r)^n − 1)

With P = ₹50,000, r = 1% and n = 10, the EMI is about ₹5,279. Total repaid ≈ ₹52,791, so interest ≈ ₹2,791.

Flat interest versus reducing balance

Flat interest is charged on the original loan amount for the whole tenure, even as principal is repaid. It looks simple, but it costs much more than the same stated rate on a reducing balance.

Same loan, flat 12%

Interest = ₹50,000 × 12% × 10/12 = ₹5,000. Total repaid = ₹55,000, or ₹5,500 every month.

That is almost double the ₹2,750 charged on a reducing balance. The true annual cost of this flat 12% works out to roughly 21% a year.

Always label the method clearly when comparing offers: "12% flat" and "12% reducing" are very different prices.

Irregular or paused principal payments

If a member pauses principal or pays a different amount, the later balances and interest must be recalculated from that month. The SHG calculator lets you edit the principal in each row of the schedule. It assumes the entered principal is paid at the end of each month and charges interest on the opening balance; your group's actual rules may differ.

Group corpus and bank linkage (CCL)

A group's corpus estimate depends on which balances are counted, whether loans outstanding are treated as group assets, grants received, liabilities and the current scheme rules. The calculator separates estimated savings, net assets and cash-like funds, and shows Cash Credit Limit (CCL) figures only as indicative planning estimates.

Banks decide the real sanction and drawing power after appraisal under current rules, so treat any estimate as a conversation starter, not a promise.

Step-by-step: planning a member loan

  1. Agree the loan amount and the purpose with the group.
  2. Fix the annual rate and state whether it is reducing or flat.
  3. Choose the tenure and the repayment method (equal principal or constant EMI).
  4. Generate the schedule in the calculator and share it with the member.
  5. Record every payment and recalculate promptly if a payment changes.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Comparing a flat rate with a reducing rate as if they were the same.
  • Charging interest on the original amount after principal has been repaid.
  • Not writing down the method, so members dispute it later.
  • Treating a calculator's CCL estimate as an approved bank limit.

Frequently asked questions

Is SHG loan interest reducing or flat?

It depends on the group's own rules. Many groups charge interest on the reducing balance, which is fairer to borrowers. Whatever method is used, it should be written down and applied the same way for every member.

How is monthly interest on an SHG loan calculated?

Divide the annual rate by 12 to get the monthly rate, then multiply it by the outstanding principal at the start of the month. At 12% a year on ₹50,000, month-one interest is ₹50,000 × 1% = ₹500.

Which is cheaper: equal principal or constant EMI?

For the same rate and tenure, equal principal costs slightly less in total interest (₹2,750 against ₹2,791 in the worked example) because principal falls faster. The trade-off is a larger instalment in the early months.

What happens if a member misses a month's principal?

The outstanding balance stays higher, so the next month's interest is higher too. Recalculate from that month onward and apply any penalty only as your group's approved rules allow.

What does CCL mean for an SHG?

CCL stands for Cash Credit Limit, a working-capital limit a bank may sanction to a group after appraisal. Any figure in a calculator is only an indicative planning estimate, not a sanction.

Does the calculator guarantee a bank loan or sanction?

No. Banks decide the actual limit after reviewing records, repayment history, group rules and current scheme guidelines.

Important note

Always verify current DAY-NRLM, State SRLM and bank instructions. Rates, eligibility, CCL and interest-subvention terms may change by financial year, lender, state and borrower category. Figures here are educational estimates, not financial advice.
