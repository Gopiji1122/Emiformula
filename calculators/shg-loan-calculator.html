Mathematical Formulas for SHG Member Lending & DAY-NRLM Bank Linkage

Self-Help Groups (SHGs / Bachat Gats) operating under the Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana – National Rural Livelihoods Mission (DAY-NRLM) and NABARD SHG-Bank Linkage framework rely on three core mathematical models verified on this page:

  • Equal Principal + Monthly Reducing Interest Method: Each month, the member repays a fixed principal slice P_month = Loan / N plus interest calculated strictly on the remaining opening balance: Interest_t = Opening_Balance_t × (r_monthly / 100). At 1% per month (₹1 सैकड़ा / 12% nominal p.a.) on ₹20,000 for 10 months, the first installment is ₹2,200 (₹2,000 principal + ₹200 interest) and decreases by ₹20 every month to ₹2,020 in Month 10, resulting in total interest of exactly ₹20,000 × 0.01 × (10 + 1) / 2 = ₹1,100.
  • Group Interest Spread (Bank CCL vs. Internal Lending): When an SHG draws from a Bank Cash Credit Limit (CCL) at 7% p.a. (under government interest subvention) and lends internally to members at 1% per month (12% p.a.) on a reducing balance, the group retains the ~5% p.a. interest differential inside the group corpus to build shared community equity.
  • Corpus & Cash Credit Limit (CCL) Multiples: Under NABARD and DAY-NRLM appraisal norms, total group corpus comprises accumulated member thrift savings, Revolving Fund (RF of ₹15,000–₹30,000), retained interest earnings, and Community Investment Fund (CIF). Banks typically assess 1st-dose to 4th-dose CCL drawing limits from 4× to 8× of the group corpus or based on the Micro Credit Plan (MCP).

Important assumptions

FAQ

Is this an official SHG loan eligibility checker?

No. CCL and corpus results are indicative planning estimates only. A bank and the relevant SHG federation or mission determine eligibility and sanction under current rules.

What does ₹1 per hundred per month mean?

It means 1% interest per month, equivalent to a nominal 12% per year before considering how the balance reduces.

Why does interest change each month?

Under reducing-balance methods, interest is calculated on the unpaid principal at the start of each month.

Does the gross interest spread equal group profit?

No. It excludes fees, timing differences, defaults, collection delays, operating expenses and any differences in actual bank drawdown or repayment.
